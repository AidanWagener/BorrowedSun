# Volume 04b — Chronology, transmission and the uses of memory

**Working canon, edition 0.7 · present 742 AL.** This is an original fictional history and an explicit continuity decision for the twelve city dossiers. Read it beside [The work we inherited](04_HISTORY_OF_NACRE.md) and the [dated event register](History/nacre_chronology.json). Dates are local orbital years of approximately 325.191 Earth days. Biological ages belong to a different calculation using Earth-standard years; a date difference of forty AL is not automatically a forty-year-old person.

The register contains 105 dated entries: 36 established master anchors, 56 bounded local adoptions, four adoptions with corrected historical attribution, and nine retained proposals. It also records ten historical eras, twenty-one relationships between events, four explicitly undated contexts and six city-source corrections. These counts describe a reconciled foundation, not every event experienced on Nacre. A chronology becomes less useful if ordinary lives are forced to fit only the things a city office remembered to name.

## 1. What a date establishes

Three records can share a year without describing the same thing. A household may occupy a bank, a surveyor may describe its structures later, and an assembly may recognize the neighborhood later still. The earliest surviving inventory establishes that an inventory existed and that its listed objects or claims were recorded. It does not establish that no person lived there before the inventory. This distinction gives Nacre cities histories without requiring them to possess one official birthday.

An adopted event establishes its bounded occurrence inside the fiction. It does not endorse every subsequent interpretation. Marroway's Kept Room register belongs to 93 AL; the argument that it invented hospitality does not become true alongside it. Brin's observatory nucleus belongs to 279 AL; institutions can still disagree about whether astronomy, cold-environment research, mineral work or navigation mattered most to its establishment. The record is stronger when the rival explanations refer to the same event rather than quietly moving it to different centuries.

The city dossiers retain their original proposal language as provenance. Edition 0.7 makes the adoption decision in this volume and the register. An entry marked **adopted** is now accepted at the register's stated scope even when the source heading says “proposed.” An entry marked **retained_proposal** remains available for future design and cannot be used as a decisive historical fact. This prevents a draft heading from being mistaken for a second, competing canon and makes the editorial act visible instead of silently changing every source.

Several precision classes are necessary. A year identifies an event without inventing its month. An interval can describe construction, adaptation or several decisions without claiming uninterrupted activity. An earliest attestation dates surviving evidence. A context window places material within an era while leaving its actual proceedings unresolved. The Relay Winter has an established onset and an unknown common end; its null endpoint is not shorthand for continuing to 742. The present population estimate and Sunseed story opening have reference epochs, not invented census dates or completed outcomes.

The ten era boundaries are an explanatory arrangement. A Tern repair program running from 452 to 477 crosses the boundary between broken links and repair. Its workers did not stop in 468 to let historians change chapter headings. Likewise, Morrow settlement phases can begin in one era and continue into the next. Historical periodization must accommodate the work it describes.

## 2. Households moved before identities settled

The three large arrival waves—approximately 72,000 people in 0 AL, 81,000 in 17 and 57,000 in 43—remain reconstructed counts. They do not define three ethnic populations with exclusive skills, faiths or temperaments. A maintenance crew, a household association, a transport sponsor and a classroom could already combine people from different origins. Arriving together could create solidarity, dependency, resentment or only a shared inconvenient memory. Later households carried these relationships forward unevenly.

The useful continuity is between ways of moving and ways of keeping a home. Orison's 9 AL freight station made a sheltered transfer possible. It did not instantly create the present city or its modern Concession. Marroway's river growth in 62–128 made household membership stretch along routes. The 93 Kept Room arrangement formalized return and belongings across several households. A person could leave a room without ceasing to belong to its obligations, and could belong to an arrangement without occupying one address forever. This later supplies material for disputes over departure; it should also supply ordinary scenes of a guest finding the correct cupboard without asking.

Hesh's East Bank occupation window remains 104–121 as a proposal. Its uncertain boundaries matter more than whether a ceremonial anniversary could be chosen. Hesh is beside a closed basin, with its inherited river terminal well away from the city center. Lake boats, overland approaches and independent crews make different kinds of journey possible. Nothing in the early settlement claim creates a convenient navigable river through the modern origin or an outlet to the ocean. A family arriving by one route can maintain a household mythology that overlooks the work of another route. That omission is a subject for history, not permission to move the geography.

Serein's durable settlement belongs broadly to 129–216; its Common Quay register of 164 provides a narrower attestation. Sola's durable Orrel harbor begins in 147, while the Veya lease of 176 concerns a later harbor relationship. These two kinds of source should sit beside one another. Serein's register names occupancy categories; Sola's lease combines lodging, wages and water-storage shares. Neither document describes a whole civilization. Both reveal how people translated a temporary arrival into a position from which they could plan a future.

The Morrow crossings intensified after 163. That marker neither excludes earlier visits nor compresses later settlement into one fleet. Talweir's 181 Gauge Rooms, Aven's 194 Shelter Ledger and the proposed Merrow and Dhal occupation phases belong to different landscapes and institutions. Talweir connects two freshwater systems with distinct levels and habitats. Aven's shelters serve an upland transfer network. Dhal and Merrow occupy separate working rivers. A familiar domestic practice could pass among their inhabitants through marriage, apprenticeship, a copied plan or a sequence of journeys; it could not drift down one imaginary continental canal connecting all four cities.

The arrival at a different sky was memorable without being a permanent psychological division. A Kesh-born traveler could miss Rauth from much of Morrow. A child raised under that absence could find the first near-side view astonishing, oppressive, beautiful or less interesting than the vessel window. Families preserved different comparisons. Historical dialogue should allow people to disagree about an experience without converting hemisphere into personality.

The later Sere and Pale histories keep the same plurality. Tern's proposed consolidation in 276–303 can precede the established 305–391 era of harbor expansion. Velis's 329 receiving arrangement records cooperation among cultivation and transport households, including losses later publicity preferred to omit. Brin's 279 observatory precedes a proposed 296–314 phase of household consolidation. None of these places is simply a remote workplace waiting centuries to acquire actual families. Conversely, a durable technical installation does not prove that every modern family descends from its first staff.

These relationships leave room for small later offworld migrations and continuing interplanetary heritage. They do not require instantaneous interstellar contact to explain each innovation. A household can treasure an offworld object whose route is documented while speaking a language shaped by several centuries on Nacre. Its story need not be either untouched ancestry or complete cultural amnesia.

## 3. Compatibility changed what people could keep

The instrument era is often represented by tall equipment and precise marks. Its domestic history is equally consequential. Marroway's Dry Door standard of 226 made selected vulnerable components replaceable. Hesh's shade-span standards of 263 connected different supports and panels. Talweir's proposed Bend Works phase, 238–251, concerns a usable navigation passage beside a flowing channel; the modern gates are later. These histories explain mixed generations of material that still work together. They do not require all old machinery to remain untouched or all modern things to look unrelated to their predecessors.

A replaceable part can change a household's bargaining position. It may let a resident commission a repair from several makers, maintain one cherished panel or improve a room gradually. It can also give a standards body power over who may certify the replacement. The history should therefore include access to tools and knowledge alongside specification numbers. Aven's Bench Schools, adopted as a 267–282 development, join adult technical learning and household education. They do not retrospectively make every learner welcome. A capable person could be excluded by hours, fees, language, care responsibilities or somebody's judgment of who looked like a future technician.

Orison's sequence is specific. A major collector enters service in 231; the Second Dawn agreements finance a connected package in 248; the Terrace Settlement forms the modern charter in 506. Light redirection, electrical supply, storage and ownership are different parts of that story. The ancient crater does not retain useful impact heat merely because that would give the city's name a satisfying explanation. Local thermal occurrences and later investigation remain available, including bounded fictional uses where subsequently authored. Neither master history nor the current city budget establishes a proven city-scale electrical geothermal resource. Historical attribution and engineering capacity must not impersonate one another.

Mirror Ring's 318 service annex follows an orbital history. It moves. Later rebuilds replace much of the hardware while a name and some obligations survive. A dock worker's family can describe three generations “working at the Ring” without all three inhabiting the same physical module. A salvaged component can return to a ground workshop; its ownership and route then need a history. This is an unusually strong setting for objects whose identity lies partly in replacement: what persists may be a service, a pattern of work, a contested claim or a particular piece of metal, and these need not coincide.

On Morrow, the 271 comparable ribbon records concern particular tissue fractions and preparation failures within the regional research history. They are not the discovery that native life is generally edible. Sola's 263 cultivation courts concern maintained introduced stocks, clean media and partner cultures. Velis's later records concern the survival and recovery of living material. Long-night-selected staple lines belong to the fiction's bounded biological premise; a date of adoption does not verify a real-world crop yield or eliminate nutrients, water, heat and clean-stock work.

The history of domestic form also follows use. Talweir's Court Extensions of 306–349 add rooms and services behind banks; Merrow's broad-roof rebuilding of 324–339 spreads repair arrangements without one taste becoming mandatory. Dhal's 309 Common Dry Store agreement can produce several successor organizations rather than one ancient office unchanged to 742. These distinctions help an environment artist build a room with a history: an inherited service wall, a younger roof, a chosen finish and an ordinary object bought last turn. Age is not a uniform material pasted over everything.

## 4. Art traveled because people wanted it

The First Wrong House is attested by a surviving script in Sola in 198. Its circulation belongs to the history of crossing as surely as rescue agreements do. The play is portable in several senses: a script can be copied, a scene recalled, an ending changed to suit an audience and an amateur performance mounted in a room never designed as a theater. Its recognizable structure can persist through different pronunciation, costumes and jokes. The history need not identify one uninterrupted “authentic” company in order for a performance to matter.

The earliest attestation does not settle the play's own prehistory. A later producer can claim an ancestor supplied a famous line; an archive may preserve only a much younger copy. That creates bounded disagreement, not an invitation to make every date arbitrary. A surviving script can establish what it contains. It cannot establish that nobody improvised a related joke before it was written.

Serein's Oblique Rooms exhibition of 337 creates a more explicit transmission chain. In 341 Marroway's Windowboat exhibitions acknowledge it among several influences. Their interest shifts toward rooms that rearrange for travel, sleeping and receiving visitors. Hesh's Small Rooms season of 604 again acknowledges the older movement while changing scale, density, dress and the effect of one carefully made object. The connection is more interesting than assigning all three cities the same decorative kit. A diagram travels readily; a domestic arrangement must survive a different room, climate, budget and household.

That chain should not become the only origin of every asymmetrical composition on Nacre. Some forms arise independently; some are practical compromises; some are deliberate rejections of fashion. A Hesh maker may know the exhibition history and still prefer symmetry. A Marroway customer can buy the scandalously expensive cupboard because it is beautiful and slightly ridiculous. Cultural transmission includes vanity, affection, commercial strategy and boredom as well as social argument.

Talweir's Four Landing Circuit of 512 changes touring practice through compatible docking. It standardizes an interface, not a repertoire. Six Returns, released by Emet Ral and collaborators in 563, later provides phrases that home musicians and commercial performers use differently. Its political readings can be serious without exhausting the recordings. A wedding performance, an unpopular arrangement or a complaint about an instrument belongs to the work's history. Ral is the only named historical creator carried forward here; no birth, death or sole-genius claim is invented for the people register.

Velis's Open Garden season of 553 and Merrow's Shared Supper circuits of 522 organize invitations rather than founding all hospitality. Scheduling can make a pleasure more accessible and more commercial at the same time. A visitor might enjoy an expensive interpretation without treating it as the authentic heir. A household might decline to open a beautiful garden because it would rather have a quiet turn. The city remains culturally alive when its people can refuse to perform themselves for visitors.

The Left Shoe printing in Velis in 638, Merrow's Paper Mile recording season in 687 and Brin's Pocket Weather craze in 716 give collecting and popular taste their own chronology. The printing elaborates an existing game anecdote. The recording season is not the invention of corner-pop. The miniature craze is not the first miniature. These distinctions prevent a catalogue of important firsts from replacing the much richer history of things becoming fashionable, losing prestige, being rediscovered and remaining someone's private favorite.

Communication and travel must remain separate. A recording from Merrow can delight listeners in Sola long before its performers or a fragile instrument arrive. Tern and Velis can exchange near-immediate race commentary while a physical sea journey takes many civil turns. Brin's Nola Iven can build a deliberately affectionate Sola miniature from a friend's recordings without ever visiting. The historical account does not correct her exaggerated pastry stall into a survey error; it preserves her purpose and labels the model accordingly.

## 5. The Relay Winter did not swallow seventy-seven years

The Relay Winter begins in 392 AL. Its established causes are a sequence of equipment failures, scheduling errors and disputes over replacement components. Different interruptions last different lengths of time. No canonical common end has been supplied. The broader broken-links era runs from 392 through 468 and includes the subsequent institutions, closures, movements and local responses. A source may rhetorically call this whole inheritance “the Winter,” but authorial chronology must identify what its speaker is compressing.

Four city entries required explicit correction: Marroway's 411 Two Copies practice, Serein's 409–416 borrowed-pattern shops, Sola's 407–414 separated ledgers and Hesh's 414–426 condensate-work departures. Their dates remain. Their context is now later broken links rather than a single initial interruption lasting decades. Merrow's 407–436 divided departures and Brin's 423–447 repair winters already made the useful distinction. Velis explicitly separated the initial event from its larger era.

The difference changes what places contain. Hesh's works close in sections, are stripped, sealed and partly reused. A functioning item left there requires a reason; the setting does not owe the player a perfectly stocked centuries-old factory. Merrow's crews make different decisions about leaving and taking equipment. A disputed acquisition can involve valid costs, coercive bargaining and opportunism without condemning every descendant. Brin's receiver shell can surround several later installations. Its current diagnostic transmitter is evidence of current work, not proof that an ancient system never stopped.

Dhal's transfer expansion in 397–421 is equally important. Local freight can grow while distant support deteriorates. A workshop may prosper because crews need substitution; a household may gain a room from someone who leaves voluntarily. The same period can contain preventable loss, successful adaptation, art, births, festivals and an investment that performs well. Erasing those trajectories would flatten twelve cities into copies of one catastrophe.

Velis's Split Return of 419–431 gives the period another kind of survival. A crop-associated practice returns through exchanges among three settlements after an earlier local form is lost. A bakery retains a household name. A genetic file might clarify ancestry without settling whether the bread's identity lies in a recipe or in the social practice of making it. The adopted fact is recovery through exchange; the dispute about identity remains. A future story should allow a more accurate label and an enjoyable loaf to coexist.

The anonymous forwarding-ledger entry of 397 remains verbatim in the master history. Its writer asks whether a sister opened a message rather than accepting the returning boat as proof of delivery. This is not a census of the era's misery. It is one precise difference between dispatch, transport and human receipt. Sola's later Three Receipts collection makes a related distinction without asserting that every failed transfer was deliberate fraud. Some accounts end in an agreed cancellation and relief.

Public reading helps explain one attributed Night Choir origin inside the era. That origin is a context window, not a founding ceremony dated to 392. Local radio, ships, surviving relays and ordinary conversation continue. The institutional inheritance concerns who can inspect, hear and challenge information when channels differ. It does not require Nacre to become technologically primitive or perpetually suspicious of every public message.

## 6. Repair included the right to enjoy a place

The 481 Open Ledger settlement is a family of agreements. It supplies a language for challenging concealed reserves without creating one planetary constitution. Its later context includes the Concession's 506 charter, but also arrangements whose most visible result is a useful room or a journey home. Repair history should contain competent provision and pleasure alongside oversight.

Hesh's open-yard settlement and Velis's Separate Copies agreement both belong to 487. Their coincidence is not a hidden common treaty. Hesh's limited safe-conduct gives crews a place to arrange repair on known terms; this is part of the world's independent travel and adventure, with no promise that every captain is harmless. Velis's agreement concerns recoverable holdings, independent backups and the recurring labor of testing them. Calling both “open” would conceal how different their objects and obligations are.

Sola's Return Seat agreement of 492 treats the end of a meeting as a transport problem. Marroway's Moving School Accord of 493 recognizes learning that follows routes. Serein's Long Rooms agreement of 501 organizes bathing, laundry, quiet and guest space. These are linked by a concern for what participation actually takes, not by proof of one coordinating authority. People need a return journey, a recognized record, a place to wash and someone whose work maintains it. A public room can be successful enough that its history becomes almost invisible to a person using it comfortably.

Talweir's 512 circuit and Merrow's 522 suppers add enjoyable movement. A stage visits a small landing because equipment and scheduling make it feasible; a diner crosses a bank because someone extends an invitation worth accepting. Brin's 512 departing households belongs to an entirely separate local story about institutional contracts and specialist choices. Some people leave gladly, some reluctantly, some return. A city's response can invest in teaching without declaring that every beneficiary must stay forever.

This history should be present in private rooms as well as public facilities. A long-used guest shelf, a repaired washing fixture or a cupboard arranged around a returning relative can reveal an institution's consequences. Such objects need not all be collectible evidence. Sometimes their meaning is that a place continues working while the traveler passes through it.

## 7. Departure, inheritance and passage are different freedoms

The Unbinding decisions of 571–589 limit or abolish inherited labor claims in several jurisdictions. They do not erase all contracts at once. The newly adopted local events give those limits shape. Sola's 582 decision rejects one class of personal landing service inherited by descent while keeping specified property contributions. Serein's 584 substitute-duty decision concerns a maintenance category in participating cooperatives. Marroway's 586 Leave Without Forfeiture distinguishes personal possessions from departure and from shared reserves.

These decisions can make meaningful freedom possible while leaving difficult obligations. A household member may own a tool personally while sharing responsibility for a dependent person's care. An owner may owe a published maintenance contribution without being entitled to demand a descendant's bodily labor. Later employers can attempt to recreate coercion through training debt or accommodation ties. Each situation needs its actual terms; neither a universal slavery law nor universal emancipation provides a shortcut.

The Passage cases are separate. They arise in Aven's upland communities within the 549–624 era and concern enclosure of refuge routes and reliable water access. The era bounds do not describe one seventy-six-year trial. The precise proceedings and litigants remain to be authored. Their later influence on coastal access law is established in principle. Dhal's industrial parcels and Tern's working waterfronts supply possible reception histories, not retroactive proof that Aven issued a planetary judgment.

The city-source corrections now preserve that distinction. A route through a yard, a right to enter a working floor, an emergency refuge and a revocable service approach can carry different terms. Present public routes should remain ordinary usable routes; a player should not have to litigate every doorway. Particular seams are interesting because most movement around them is possible. The freedom to explore gains force when alternate approaches have real destinations and constraints, rather than every invitation concealing a bureaucratic denial.

## 8. Ecological memory is not a single repeating disaster

The 76 contamination, 367–372 Mothglass controversy and 612 Blue Sill hearings have different causes and scales. The first combines drainage decisions with wet conditions. Mothglass combines fraud with an actual ecological problem. Blue Sill links cultivated-water discharge to nursery damage and the prior observations of people whose warnings carried less authority. They are not three names for the same lesson that every technological intervention must fail.

Merrow's proposed 614–650 recovery agreements remain a phase proposal. The hearings are established; individual obligations, nursery sites and recovery boundaries still need detailed authorship. The modern museum is a later institution. It cannot automatically identify the original hearing room or every damaged habitat. A present estuary bank intervention, including the separately tracked MOR-E04 question, needs its own construction date and causal account. A later harmful action may occur despite knowledge of older harm without being literally the same discharge continuing for a century.

Dhal's 667 Wet Rooms inquiry and Hesh's 688 Saltward review provide material histories at another scale. Dhal separates accelerated product claims, installation errors and misplaced household blame. Hesh connects residues and future storage that earlier accounts treated separately. Neither means nobody understood roofs or salt before the inquiry. Expertise can be substantial and still fail when obligations are split, evidence is overstated or one attractive account leaves costs somewhere else.

Recent adaptation also contains successful work. Dhal's 701–718 Canopy Connection revises observation routes when early designs undervalue habitat. Sola's 731 Neri setback moves selected paths and rooms without emptying an island. Velis's 713–719 Broken Branch closures include new uses and households that remain. Their inhabitants have memories, ownership and future plans. Calling every closed structure a ruin available to whoever arrives would erase the very depth the world is trying to build.

## 9. The present is built from overlapping improvements

Talweir's Level Door program runs 689–707; Aven's Roof and Rest work 698–716; Dhal's Canopy Connection 701–718; Tern's Second Life program 704–721. These intervals overlap but do not establish a single planetary modernization campaign. They respond to different terrains, advocates, equipment, budgets and earlier compromises. People using mobility devices, caregivers, injured workers, freight handlers and residents wanting an easier route can share interests without having identical needs.

A new ramp does not invent disabled people. A revised route should imply older ways of managing, including competent adaptations, exclusion and workarounds residents are glad to stop using. Nor does adopting a historical program certify every future level as accessible. Its consequences must become actual route geometry, transfer equipment, signs, seating and dependable alternatives. The date tells an artist which layers might meet; it does not solve their implementation.

Brin's shared apprenticeship rooms of 731 and Serein's 728 roof revision concern other kinds of access. A graduate can accept an opportunity elsewhere. A household can preserve its garden's privacy while supporting a public connection. A person can care deeply about a city and still find its celebrated institution irritating. These positions give present characters something more specific to inherit than faction loyalty.

The 698 population synthesis estimates about 31 million. The current working estimate of 34.8 million belongs to the 742 reference context with uncertainty; this volume does not invent a fresh census taken precisely in 742. Twelve city-core populations describe only a fraction of that world. Later demographic writing must account for smaller settlements, rural networks, mobile populations, differing administrative boundaries and revised counting. A local visitor book such as Sola's 694 reform can change an account without moving any actual human being.

The Sunseed affair begins inside this inherited world. Its authorization lineage can have extraordinary consequences because services, claims and alternatives already exist. It does not create infinite power or reveal that all twelve cities were secretly one concession. A person in Merrow can have an absorbing life whose central concern is unrelated to Orison. The larger story becomes more convincing when it reaches such people through actual effects rather than announcing that they have always been waiting for its protagonist.

## 11. How evidence becomes a place

Every important historical environment should separate occurrence, surviving material, remembered story and present purpose. A room may commemorate a 198 script while its oldest wall dates from a later rebuilding. A repaired 231 foundation may carry no original moving machinery. A 93 agreement may survive in a careful copy held in a busy present institution. A monument erected long after a dispute tells a second history about its sponsors. These layers should be legible through construction, labels, conversation and use, not only a collectible paragraph.

Custody supplies useful adventure. An object can move by a documented purchase, salvage, gift, loan, theft or emergency evacuation. A gap in its record invites inquiry without proving wrongdoing. Returning it may restore one claim while disappointing another. Conversely, some objects have straightforward histories and should remain ordinary. If every decorated box reveals a buried crime, decoration ceases to express taste and becomes an interrogation prompt.

The two original archival passages in Volume 04 remain unchanged. The Arrival School teaching copy has uncertain attribution and no established copying date; its position in an early chapter is not a date. The anonymous ledger entry is dated 397 but has no invented writer or route. Their different certainty must survive adaptation into voiced dialogue or an in-world display. An actor can give a reading warmth without silently supplying a verified identity.

Present residents are more than custodians of these sources. A family may prefer a working door to restoration of its handsome damaged predecessor. A boat owner may keep a famous cabinet in a private room. An archivist can find a better explanation without settling what a revived bread means to the household eating it. An independent captain may tell an entertaining account of an acquisition that other records complicate. The place becomes deep when people have reasons to care, disagree, ignore, preserve or change what they inherited—and can continue living after the traveler leaves.

## Source and validation record

This edition reconciles original repository fiction, not external historical claims. The event register cites passage anchors in [Volume 04](04_HISTORY_OF_NACRE.md) and all twelve city dossiers. Its interpretation also respects [languages and belonging](05_LANGUAGES_NAMES_AND_BELONGING.md), [power and exchange](06_POWER_AND_EXCHANGE.md), the [regional atlas](07_REGIONAL_ATLAS.md), the societies foundation and the pleasures volume. No outside source is being presented as evidence that these fictional events occurred.

The dedicated `Tools/World/check_chronology.py` checks event identity, dates, precision, source paths and anchors, atlas location references, relations, era coverage, protected master dates and the two original archival passages. Its negative controls exercise incorrect dates, invented Relay Winter closure, missing provenance, bad references and path escape. These checks can catch continuity damage; they cannot prove that an interpretation is artistically satisfying or that a historical building is playable.
