# Borrowed Sun: the living planet bible

**Authoring in progress.** This directory is the new authoritative home for the full-world project requested on 13 October 2026. It does not yet contain the completed planet bible or a constructed planet. The [ordinary-prop integration](../Operations/CITY_OBJECTS_2026-10-13.md) has passed its Development handoff; the active phase is now deep worldbuilding. Planet construction follows completion and coherence review of the full canon and lore.

Read [canon reconciliation](CANON_RECONCILIATION.md) and the [initial research ledger](RESEARCH_FOUNDATIONS.md) next. The unchanged [original game specification](Sources/Original_Game_Specification_2026-10-12.md) is preserved as a historical design source, not presented as newly written planet lore.

The world must make exploration compelling at street, regional and planetary scales. Its geography, societies, wildlife and histories must explain one another. A place should offer more than a view: something to understand, a person or creature with an independent purpose, an opportunity to act, or a reason to return when circumstances change. Distance must communicate a large inhabited world while journeys offer decisions and discoveries.

## The requested outcome

Create a substantive multi-volume bible amounting to hundreds of readable pages, supported by a geographic atlas and linked production records. Length is evidence of scope only. Repetition, empty headings, mechanically expanded lists and name-only catalogues do not meet the depth requirement. Publication-quality art and the AAA ambition require subsequent work in the playable world; prose alone cannot certify them.

The planet-scale setting includes multiple distinct cities, climatic regions and biomes; natural and engineered light cycles; changing weather; ecosystems and animal behavior; interconnected cultures, politics and economies; consequential travel; named people and social networks; hundreds of specific points of interest; and dynamic events with causes, boundaries and persistent aftermath. Gameplay systems will be derived from this world rather than used to arbitrarily constrain its invention.

## Canon and decision authority

1. The user's explicit decisions and latest scope changes have priority.
2. Approved art anchors remain Brass Sanctuary, Indigo Monsoon and the original moth-shaped Lantern Keeper.
3. The existing game specification and asset bible are prior design sources. Many passages explicitly describe proposals. Preserve valuable fiction while recording conflicts and revisions rather than silently declaring every old production limit permanent canon.
4. Implemented prototype behavior is evidence of the build, not automatic truth about every place on the future planet.
5. New fiction becomes the working canon through explicit decisions recorded with their dependencies. Research supports plausibility; it does not establish that a fictional civilization or planet exists.

The original specification locates Orison on a tidally locked moon. The initial reconciliation retains an inhabited world at planetary scale and investigates its host-planet relationship before fixing astronomical parameters. The user used "planet" to describe the full explorable world; that does not by itself require silently deleting the earlier moon setting. The star, parent body, radius, gravity, calendars and climate remain open until their combined model is checked.

## Depth requirements

Every regional dossier needs causes for its terrain and climate, a resource base, water and food sources, settlement distribution, historical layers, political relationships, endemic and shared life, seasonal changes, visual/audio signatures, routes and departures from neighboring regions. A palette swap is not a region.

Every city needs a reason to exist at its location, an account of its food/water/energy/waste, a history expressed in its layout, varied neighborhoods, institutions that disagree internally, a plausible workday, distinctive approaches from land and air, routes through multiple elevations, and places that residents use without the player. Not every culture is an extension of Orison, and not every problem is a daylight lease.

Each authored point of interest must explain why it is here, who or what uses it, what can be inferred on arrival, what the player can do, what changes with time or circumstances, where its clues lead, why a return can matter, and which concrete assets it needs. "Ancient ruin with loot" fails this standard.

Species need ecological roles and energy sources, lifecycles, senses, reproduction, niches, predators/competitors, interactions with settlement and weather, readable behavior and limits. Predators cannot constitute most of an ecosystem. Domesticated life, pests, decomposers and microscopic processes shape the world even when they do not all receive simulated actors.

Named people need overlapping relationships and commitments beyond the player. A schedule must adapt to a closed workplace, a festival, weather, bereavement or danger without making the person omniscient. A faction has institutions, disagreements, resources, recruitment, constraints and a history; it is not a costume and a morality score.

Dynamic events require a cause and an aftermath. A flood affects routes, supplies, habitats, repairs and rumors through identified connections. It does not instantly update every remote city. Recovery and quiet periods must exist alongside crises. Events cannot consume irreplaceable authored content without a deliberate world-state decision.

## Authoring sequence and completion gates

First reconcile existing canon and assemble checked primary research. Next establish the physical model and chronology. Build the atlas, ecological and settlement networks together so their dependencies can challenge one another. Develop cultures and institutions across those networks, then deepen regions, cities, people, routes and POIs. Write events from the resulting causal relationships. Derive the asset and construction plan from the settled world.

Before planet construction begins, all volumes must contain substantive finished content; geographic references and dates must reconcile; major settlements must have viable supply networks; climate and ecology must have explicit assumptions; journeys must have checked scale/time implications; and cross-references must resolve. A readable compiled edition and atlas must be reviewed. Content counts and word counts will be reported as counts, not quality certification.

Construction then proceeds through demonstrable slices of the canonical world with reproducible recipes and saved-state contracts. Planetary scale, arbitrary landing, spherical gravity, terrain collision, weather and population simulation each require actual engine evidence. A globe render is an atlas/presentation artifact until those capabilities are implemented and tested.

## Working boundaries

The initial asset/tool budget remains under EUR 500. Use original art and suitable premade packs when they improve the result. Keep shortlist relevance separate from purchase, account rights and operation-specific AI processing terms. The existing 4,200 asset briefs are planning inputs, not finished geometry or proof that every future biome is covered.

No new combat, inventory, parkour or destruction implementation begins during the worldbuilding phase. Their future environmental needs belong in the bible. Preserve existing mechanics and validated builds. No claim of a completed AAA world, finished assets or successful gameplay may be inferred from written plans.
